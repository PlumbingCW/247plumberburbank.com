Basement Waterproofing in Burbank, IL

A basement that takes on water after heavy rain has either a water table problem, a drainage problem at the foundation or a sewer backup. Burbank 24/7 Plumbing looks at the plumbing side of the problem and the drainage around the house.

The first job is working out where the water is coming from, because each source needs a different fix.

Find the Source Before You Pay for a Fix.

Water comes into basements in three main ways: through floor cracks and wall joints from groundwater, through the floor drain from a backed-up sewer, or through pipe and fixture leaks. A sump pump solves the first, a backwater valve solves the second and a repair solves the third.

Describe what you see. Water seeping at the wall base after rain points to groundwater. Dirty water at the floor drain points to the sewer.

What the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District Advises.

The Metropolitan Water Reclamation District of Greater Chicago publishes flood guidance for residents. It recommends disconnecting downspouts, conserving water during storms, signing up for Overflow Action Alerts and documenting damage. It also notes the homeowner owns the private pipe and should hire a licensed professional. The MWRD number in its guide is 312.751.6633.

Those steps are a good starting point for any wet basement in Burbank.

What We Do About a Wet Basement.

Waterproofing here means managing water, since a basement is below the water table in many parts of the Chicago area.

Interior drain and sump.

A perimeter drain collects water at the footing and routes it to a sump pit and pump. See sump pump installation for the pump side of the system.

Downspout and grade review.

Downspouts that discharge at the foundation drive water into the soil. We check and advise on extensions and grade.

Backwater valve.

A valve on the house sewer closes when the main surcharges, which keeps sewage out of floor drains during heavy rain.

Floor drain and cleanout work.

We clear and repair floor drains and cleanouts that let water in or hold it back.

Planned Flood Work Nearby and What It Means for You.

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan describes a planned expansion of the Melvina Ditch Reservoir run by the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District, with up to 195 acre-feet of added storage. The plan gives no completion date, so it should not be treated as protection today.

Until public projects are finished and proven, a working sump system and a backwater valve are the practical protections for a basement.

Steps Homeowners Can Take This Season.

Several low-cost steps reduce basement water risk.

  • Extend downspouts so they discharge at least a few feet from the house.
  • Keep the sump pit clean and test the pump before storm season.
  • Never connect a sump pump or downspout to the sanitary sewer.
  • Raise stored items on shelves or pallets.
  • Know where the cleanout and the main water shutoff are.

The South Stickney Sanitary District states that sump pumps must not be connected to the sanitary sewer.

According to the Cook County hazard mitigation plan, flooding is a recurring risk in the county, including basement flooding from sewer backup and overland water. That is the background for the options on this page.
