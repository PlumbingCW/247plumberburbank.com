Installation Is Planning Plus Pipes.

A new fixture is only as good as the supply and drain behind it. Before a sink, tub or toilet is installed, a plumber checks water supply size, drain slope and venting, because a fixture that drains slowly is usually a layout problem.

That is why we ask about the room, the existing lines and what you want to add before giving a price.

Installation Jobs We Handle.

These are the projects we install most often.

Bathroom fixtures.

Toilets, vanities, tubs, showers and valves are installed with new supply lines and shutoff valves. See toilet installation for fixture details.

Kitchen fixtures.

Sinks, faucets, dishwashers and disposals are connected with proper drains and air gaps. For disposals see garbage disposal installation.

Basement and utility additions.

Basement bathrooms need drains below the main line, and many need an ejector pump to lift waste to the sewer.

Appliance lines.

Washing machine boxes, ice maker lines and utility sinks are added with the proper valves and drains.

WaterSense and Code in Burbank.

The Illinois Department of Public Health approved Burbank's ordinances adopting the Illinois Plumbing Code in January 2016. The same ordinances require WaterSense-labeled new and replacement fixtures, which we specify on installations.

The City of Burbank Building Department requires a permit for plumbing construction or alteration. The office is at 6530 W 79th Street, and the number is 708-599-5500, option 2.

How to Prepare for an Installation Visit.

A little preparation helps the plumber give a precise price.

  • Photograph the area and the nearest shutoff valves.
  • Have the fixture model numbers or a link to the products you chose.
  • Measure the space, including clearance to walls.
  • Decide whether you want new shutoff valves, which we recommend.
  • Tell the plumber about any other trades involved, such as tile or cabinets.

We can recommend fixtures that fit your space and meet code.

What the Inspection Checks.

New fixtures and piping must follow the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard found in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890 and administered by the Illinois Department of Public Health, and the City of Burbank Building Department inspects the work. Inspectors check slope, venting, supports and water-saving fixtures.

The approved Burbank ordinances include the WaterSense requirement, so low-flow fixtures are part of the plan.

What to Do Before the Installer Arrives.

A little preparation keeps the job safe and quick.

  • Clear a path to the work area and the main shutoff.
  • Move stored items and cover nearby floors.
  • Tell the household when the water will be off.
  • Do not touch the old fixture connections yourself.
  • Keep pets and children away from open pipes and tools.

If you smell gas or see water spreading, call dispatch first.
