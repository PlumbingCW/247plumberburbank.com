When Plumbing Stops a Business.

A leaking pipe over a stockroom, a backed-up restroom or a flooded kitchen floor can close a business for the day. Shut off the water to the affected area first, then call the 24/7 line.

Tell dispatch the type of business, whether the building has a landlord or manager, and whether you can reach the shutoff. Those answers decide who authorizes the repair and how quickly we can get started.

Commercial Problems We See Most Often.

Commercial plumbing differs from residential in the volume of water and the number of fixtures sharing a line.

Floor drain backups.

Floor drains in kitchens, shops and garages carry heavy loads. Grease and debris build up faster than in a house.

Leaks in multi-unit buildings.

A leak in one tenant space often shows up on the ceiling of the unit below. Finding the source needs a section-by-section survey.

Restroom and fixture failures.

Flush valves, urinals and sinks in public restrooms see constant use and fail on a schedule.

Water heaters and boilers.

Larger tanks serve kitchens, salons and shop floors. A failure stops the business the same day.

Services for Burbank Businesses.

Commercial work in Burbank clusters along the West 79th Street corridor and Cicero Avenue, where shops, restaurants and offices share older buildings and newer strip centers such as Burbank Plaza.

Drain and sewer service.

Main-line cleaning, jetting and camera inspection for restaurants, car washes, laundromats and offices. See the drain cleaning page and the hydro jetting page for the methods.

Commercial leak detection.

Pressure testing and listening tools find leaks in risers, slab lines and mechanical rooms without tearing out finished space. Our leak detection page explains the process.

Backflow testing.

Many commercial buildings have backflow preventers on irrigation, fire and process lines. These are tested on a schedule, and a failed test must be repaired and retested.

Grease interceptors and traps.

Restaurants and food shops need regular service on grease interceptors so that fats do not reach the sewer. Records of service help during inspections.

Permits and Licensed Work in Burbank.

The City of Burbank Building Department requires a permit for the construction or alteration of plumbing, and all contractors must be licensed. The office is at 6530 W 79th Street, and the number is 708-599-5500, option 2.

The Illinois Plumbing Code applies, and Burbank requires WaterSense-labeled new and replacement fixtures under ordinances approved by the Illinois Department of Public Health in 2016. We specify compliant fixtures on commercial jobs.

Codes That Apply to Commercial Work.

According to state rules, commercial plumbing in Illinois follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard found in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890 and administered by the Illinois Department of Public Health, and backflow protection is required on many commercial water services. Grease interceptors for food service are regulated locally.

The City of Burbank Building Department can confirm permit needs for a specific address, and we plan the work around those requirements.
