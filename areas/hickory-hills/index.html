Higher Ground, Different Water Habits.

Hickory Hills sits on the Tinley Moraine at an elevation of about 680 feet, which is higher than much of the nearby flat land. It was incorporated as a village in 1951, became a city in 1966 and had a 2020 Census population of 14,505.

Homes on a moraine can still take water. Sloped lots send stormwater toward foundations, and clay soils hold water against basement walls.

City Guidance on Preventing Flooded Basements.

Hickory Hills publishes flood prevention guidance for residents. The points below come from that guidance and match what we see in the field.

  • Do not connect downspouts or sump pumps to the sewer.
  • Direct downspouts so water drains at least three feet from the house.
  • Consider an overhead sewer if the basement is prone to backups.
  • Use the MWRD free rain barrel program to capture roof water.
  • Keep sump pits clean and test the pump regularly.

For a failing pump, see our sump pump repair and installation service.

Lake Michigan Water Through a Commission.

Hickory Hills has used 100 percent Lake Michigan water since 1988, delivered through the West Suburban Water Commission, which was formerly the Justice-Willow Springs commission. The city is a downstream customer of Chicago water.

The water department can be reached through Public Works at 708-598-7855, and City Hall at 8652 W 95th Street answers at 708-598-4800. Call them first if the problem looks like a water main break on the street.
