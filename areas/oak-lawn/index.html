Point-of-Sale Sewer Inspection in Oak Lawn.

Oak Lawn's municipal code, in section 9-4D-11, requires a sewer inspection when a property is sold. Sellers who plan a listing can schedule a camera inspection ahead of time, so repairs are known before a contract is signed.

Our sewer inspection service records the line, and the footage can be shared with the buyer. If the camera finds a defect, our sewer line repair service handles it.

Storm Drains, Creeks and Basement Flooding.

Oak Lawn keeps its sanitary and storm sewers separate. The village describes more than 116 miles of storm sewer that flows to Stony Creek, Oak Lawn Creek, Melvina Ditch or an MWRD deep tunnel.

Why basements still flood.

Separate systems still overload in heavy rain. Water can back up through floor drains or seep through foundations when the creeks run high.

Flood basins.

The village and the MWRD built a flood-control basin near Polaris Intermediate School. Basins reduce but do not eliminate the risk.

What to do at home.

A working sump pump, disconnected downspouts and a backwater valve on the house sewer are the practical protections. See sump pump installation.

Before We Arrive at Your Oak Lawn Home.

A few minutes of preparation helps the plumber start quickly.

Stop the water.

Close the main water valve if a pipe is leaking, or stop using fixtures if a drain is backing up.

Clear the way.

Move items away from the water heater, floor drain or the sink cabinet so the plumber can reach it.

Note the details.

Write down which fixtures are affected and when the problem began.

Find the cleanout.

Look for a capped pipe near the basement floor drain. It is the fastest way into the line.

Landmarks and Neighborhoods Dispatch Knows.

On April 21, 1967, a tornado struck Oak Lawn and damaged about 900 buildings, according to village records. The event shaped the way the community talks about storm preparation, and it is a reason homeowners here take drainage seriously.

Permits and Codes in Oak Lawn.
