Start With How Your Household Uses Hot Water.

A heater that is too small runs out during back-to-back showers, and one that is too large wastes energy keeping extra water hot. Sizing starts with the number of people, the number of bathrooms and what runs at the same time, such as a washing machine and a dishwasher.

Tell the plumber about peak mornings and any large soaking tub. Those answers matter more than the age of the house.

Gas or Electric Tank.

Most Burbank basements already have a gas line and a flue, which makes like-for-like replacement the simplest path.

Gas tank.

  • Faster recovery after heavy use.
  • Needs a working flue and combustion air.
  • Gas line and shutoff must be sound.
  • Common in older homes.

Electric tank.

  • No flue required.
  • Simpler installation in tight spaces.
  • Needs a dedicated circuit.
  • Slower recovery on large tanks.

Switching fuel types adds work, so many installs keep the existing fuel.

Parts That Come With a Proper Install.

The tank is only part of the job.

Expansion tank.

A closed system needs a way to absorb expanded water as it heats. Without it the pressure cycles, and the relief valve can start dripping.

Venting and clearances.

Gas venting must rise and slope correctly, clear combustibles and carry exhaust out. We test the draft before leaving.

Relief valve and discharge line.

The temperature and pressure valve protects the tank. Its discharge pipe goes to a safe place near the floor.

Shutoff and drain pan.

A working shutoff on the cold supply and a pan under the tank limit damage if the unit ever fails.

Hardness and the Life of the New Tank.

Chicago supplies water to the district serving Burbank, and its 2013 quarterly lab reports listed hardness of 134 to 143 mg/L as calcium carbonate, which is about 8 grains per gallon. That is a 2013 value, so ask the utility for the current figure.

Water in that range builds scale, so plan to flush the new tank yearly and check the anode rod. A softener on the main supply is the next step for homes that see heavy scale, and our softener and filtration page explains it.

Standards for a Clean Installation.

A new water heater needs a relief valve rated to ANSI Z21.22, a discharge pipe that ends where escaping water is visible and a correct gas or electrical connection. The gas side follows NFPA 54.

The permit check follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard found in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890 and administered by the Illinois Department of Public Health.

What to Do if You Smell Gas Near the Heater.

A gas smell near a water heater is not a wait-and-see problem.

  • Do not use switches, lighters or phones near the heater.
  • Leave the building and take everyone out with you.
  • Call the gas utility from outside.
  • Do not re-enter until the utility says it is safe.
  • Call us after the gas is shut off to arrange the repair.

Never try to relight a heater when you smell gas.
