Water Is Going Somewhere: Act Before It Spreads.

If you see water on a ceiling, wall or floor and cannot find the source, shut off the main water valve. The main is usually in the basement near the meter, and shutting it stops the flow while you wait for help.

A hidden leak behind a wall causes mold and rot over days, so treat a spreading stain as urgent and call the 24/7 line.

The Meter Test That Starts Most Leak Calls.

Before any equipment comes out, a quick test tells you whether the leak is real and whether it is inside or outside the house.

1

Shut everything off.

Turn off every faucet, appliance and ice maker so no water is being used.

2

Read the meter.

Find the meter near the water heater or under a sink and note the dial or low-flow indicator.

3

Wait.

Check again after twenty or thirty minutes. Any movement means water is leaking.

4

Call with the result.

Tell dispatch whether the meter moved. It narrows the search before we arrive.

Where Hidden Leaks Show Up in Burbank Homes.

Most leaks announce themselves through a symptom rather than a puddle. These are the signs we see most.

A high water bill with no change in use.

Often a running toilet, a leaking valve or a buried supply line. The meter test tells you which side of the house it is on.

Stains on a ceiling or wall.

A pinhole in a supply pipe or a failing drain joint. Thermal imaging and moisture meters trace it without opening the drywall.

Wet floor in a basement or crawlspace.

It may be a pipe above the floor, a failed fitting or groundwater. Locating the source decides which trade fixes it.

Low pressure or the sound of running water.

A leak on the supply line to the house can show up as a hiss near the meter or a drop in pressure.

How the Source Is Found Without Tearing Things Apart.

Locating a leak is a diagnostic job before it is a repair job. The tools match the kind of leak.

Acoustic listening.

Sensitive microphones pick up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe. Along a floor or the street side of the house, the sound is loudest directly above the leak.

Thermal imaging.

A thermal camera shows temperature differences in walls and floors. Hot water leaks and radiant lines often show as warm patches.

Moisture mapping.

A moisture meter confirms how far water has traveled so that the repair opening is as small as possible.

Pressure testing.

Isolating a section of pipe and watching the gauge confirms whether the leak is in the house or on the line to the street.

Slab and Underground Leaks in Burbank Homes.

Many Burbank homes have basements rather than slab-on-grade floors, so a classic slab leak is less common here than in other parts of the country. A leak under a basement floor or in the line from the street still happens, and it is found with the same listening and pressure methods.

If a leak is on the water service line that runs from the street to the house, see the water service line repair page for the repair options.

Chicago Water Meters and Leak Alerts.

Burbank water comes through the South Stickney Sanitary District, and the district's guidance says the meter is typically inside the house near the water heater or under the sink. The district also describes a ten-minute toilet dye test, noting toilets are the top cause of lost water.

Run that dye test first. A running toilet costs far less to fix than an excavation, and it is the most common answer behind a rising water bill.

Commercial and Multifamily Leaks.

Rental buildings, small offices and shops have more pipe and more places to look. We survey the building by section, test risers and mains and report which area is leaking so the owner can decide on repair.

Sources We Use for Hidden Leaks.

According to the EPA WaterSense program, a water meter can reveal leaks, and the same method is the first test we run. A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign of a hidden loss.

Repairs that follow detection are covered by the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard found in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890 and administered by the Illinois Department of Public Health, and a permit applies when a line is replaced.

Leaks in a Multi-Unit Building.

A leak in a condo can reach the unit below, so it helps to find the source fast. Owners at Briartree and similar communities should also tell the association, because shared lines may be involved.
