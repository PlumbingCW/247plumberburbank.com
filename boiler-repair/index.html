No Heat in Winter: Safety First.

If you smell gas, leave the house immediately and call the gas utility from outside. Do not light matches or flip switches. A suspected carbon monoxide alarm also means fresh air first and a call for help second.

If the boiler is simply cold, check the thermostat, the power switch and the water pressure gauge, then call the line. Dispatch will ask what the boiler displays and when it last ran.

What We Repair on Hydronic Heating.

Many homes in the Chicago area heat with hot water through radiators or baseboard. These are the parts we work on most.

Ignition and controls.

Thermostats, aquastats, ignition modules and flame sensors fail and stop the boiler from firing.

Circulator pumps and zone valves.

A weak circulator or a stuck zone valve leaves part of the house cold while the boiler runs.

Expansion tanks and relief valves.

An expansion tank that has lost its air charge lets pressure climb until the relief valve opens.

Bleeding and pressure.

We bleed trapped air, set the pressure and check for leaks that cause repeated pressure loss.

Water Quality and Boiler Life.

Chicago, which supplies water to Burbank through the district, reported hardness of 134 to 143 mg/L as calcium carbonate in its 2013 lab reports. That is a 2013 figure, so confirm the current number with the utility.

A closed hydronic system uses the same water for years, so scale is less of an issue than in a water heater. A new boiler or a system that has been drained and refilled takes fresh water and needs flushing and treatment.

Keep the Boiler Healthy Between Calls.

A little maintenance cuts the number of no-heat calls.

  • Have the boiler serviced before the heating season starts.
  • Check the pressure gauge monthly and note any steady drop.
  • Keep the area around the boiler clear of storage.
  • Bleed radiators at the start of winter.
  • Replace carbon monoxide alarm batteries and test the unit.

If the pressure keeps dropping, call. A hidden leak in the system needs a plumber, not just a top-up.

Boiler Rules in Illinois.

Boiler construction follows the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, a national standard, and Illinois regulates boilers through the Office of the State Fire Marshal. Ask dispatch whether a commercial boiler at your address falls under that oversight.

Gas piping and venting follow NFPA 54. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility first.
