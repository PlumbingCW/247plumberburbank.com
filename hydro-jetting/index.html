A Backup That Keeps Returning Needs More Than a Cable.

A cable punches a channel through a blockage, but the grease, scale and root hairs on the pipe wall stay in place. Jetting uses a hose and a rotating nozzle to scrub the whole circumference, which is why it is the tool of choice after repeat backups.

If a floor drain is overflowing now, call the line. Jetting is scheduled quickly when a crew is free, and a cable can restore flow first if the situation needs it.

How a Jetting Job Works.

The sequence matters, because jetting on a failing pipe can do harm.

1

Locate the access.

The cleanout or a pulled toilet gives the hose a way in.

2

Look first.

3

Jet the line.

The nozzle scours the wall while the hose is withdrawn.

4

Check the result.

A second camera pass shows the clean pipe and any defect left behind.

Where Jetting Does Not Help.

Water cannot fix a pipe that has collapsed, sagged or separated at a joint. If the camera shows a break, jetting stops and the conversation moves to repair, such as spot repair or a liner.

The sewer line repair page covers those options, and the sewer inspection service explains what the camera reveals.

Know Where the House Line Ends.

The South Stickney Sanitary District maintains the public sewer mains that serve Burbank. Jetting that stops at the property line is the homeowner's job, while a clog in the main belongs to the district.

Good Candidates for Jetting.

Jetting is a maintenance and recovery tool. These situations are the usual reason for it.

  • Kitchen lines coated with cooking grease.
  • Main lines with recurring tree-root hairs.
  • Floor drains that clear for a month and then back up.
  • Commercial kitchen and restaurant drains.
  • Lines with heavy mineral scale on older iron.

Older cast iron can be thin in places, so a camera pass comes first on any pipe of unknown condition.

Where Jetting Fits in the Standards.

Hydro jetting is a cleaning method, not a repair, so it sits outside the plumbing code. What the code cares about is the condition of the pipe afterward. A camera pass that follows the NASSCO inspection standard gives a consistent way to describe cracks, roots and offsets.

Ask for the recording if the line has a history of trouble.
