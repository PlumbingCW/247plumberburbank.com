Why Inspect Before Repairing.

A repair estimate without a camera is a guess. The footage shows whether the pipe needs cleaning, a spot repair, a liner or a full replacement, and it shows where.

It also protects a buyer. A sewer line can look fine from the basement while a failure lies under the yard.

How an Inspection Works.

The process is the same for a repeat backup and a home purchase.

1

Access.

The camera enters through a cleanout, or a toilet is pulled when no cleanout exists.

2

Record.

The camera travels the line to the street connection while recording footage.

3

Locate.

A transmitter on the camera lets the crew mark the depth and the position of any defect on the surface.

4

Report.

You receive the video and a summary of findings with photos.

What the Camera Can Show.

These are the findings we report most often.

Roots.

Fine roots at joints or a heavy mass inside the pipe, with the distance from the cleanout.

Cracks and breaks.

Longitudinal cracks, broken sections and holes that let soil in.

Bellies and offsets.

A low spot that holds water or a joint that has shifted out of line.

Buildup and scale.

Grease, mineral scale and debris narrowing the bore of the pipe.

Worth knowing

Inspections When Buying or Selling a Home Nearby.

Some nearby villages require a sewer inspection at the time of sale. Oak Lawn, for example, has a point-of-sale sewer inspection requirement in its municipal code. Burbank is a separate municipality, and rules differ from town to town.

Whether or not a town requires one, buyers often choose a camera inspection as part of due diligence. A recorded run gives the buyer and seller the same facts.

Who Handles What After the Inspection.

If the camera shows damage in the house line, we explain the repair choices and quote them. If the problem is in the public main, the South Stickney Sanitary District is the responsible party, and its office number is 708-424-6030.

The camera footage helps the district understand the problem when you call. See our sewer line repair page for what happens when the footage shows a defect.

When to Schedule a Camera Inspection.

An inspection is worth the cost in these situations.

  • A drain has clogged more than once in a year.
  • You are buying an older home with a clay or cast iron sewer line.
  • There are wet patches or sunken ground along the sewer path.
  • You plan to landscape or build over the line.
  • Roots or past repairs are on the property record.

Dispatch can also schedule an inspection after a cleaning so you can see the result.

Inspection Practice and Reporting.

The NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program sets a common standard to describe pipe defects, and it is the vocabulary we use when we explain a recording. Cracks, roots, offsets and bellies are named the same way across the industry.

Ask for the recording and the notes. They are useful to a buyer, an insurer or a second plumber.

Inspecting Houses From the Mid-Century Era.

Houses on streets such as those around Treasure Island in Burbank are described in listing data as built between 1953 and 1971. We cannot say what pipes are under any street, which is why a camera inspection is the honest way to find out.
