Cleaning Drains With Vinegar and Baking Soda: Burbank Guide

The vinegar and baking soda method is popular because it is cheap, safe for most pipes and easy to try. It works for some jobs and does nothing for others.

This guide explains the difference so you can try it where it helps and call a plumber where it will not.

What the Fizz Actually Does.

Baking soda and vinegar react to form carbon dioxide, water and a mild salt. The fizz loosens a thin film of soap, grease and odor-causing residue on the pipe wall. It does not have the force to move a clump of hair or a solid plug.

Think of it as a light cleaner. It freshens a drain that smells, and it can help a sink that has only slowed a little.

Where It Helps and Where It Does Not.

Match the problem to the method before you start.

The Safe Way to Try It.

Follow these steps on a sink or tub that is slow but still draining.

1

Clear the standing water.

Remove as much water as you can from the basin.

2

Add baking soda.

Pour about half a cup of baking soda down the drain.

3

Add vinegar.

Pour in about a cup of white vinegar, cover the drain and wait fifteen minutes.

4

Flush with hot water.

Run hot tap water for a minute or two. Use hot water, not boiling, since boiling water can soften plastic pipe joints.

Worth knowing

Never Mix It With Commercial Drain Cleaner.

If you have already used a chemical drain cleaner, do not add vinegar on top of it. Mixing acids and caustic cleaners can release harmful fumes and splash hot liquid. Rinse with plenty of cold water first, and tell the plumber what you used.

Chemical cleaner sitting in a blocked pipe is also a hazard to the person who opens the trap.

Where Your Drain Responsibility Ends in Burbank.

The South Stickney Sanitary District states that it is responsible for sewer mains and that customers are responsible beyond the main, including the connection under the parkway. Anything you pour down a drain travels through your house sewer first.

A fizz treatment affects only the pipe near the fixture. If water is backing up in several fixtures, the blockage is further down in your house sewer or in the main, and the district can help check the main.

Source: South Stickney Sanitary District customer FAQ.

Better Habits Than Weekly Fizzing.

Prevention beats treatment.

  • Catch hair with a strainer in showers and tubs.
  • Pour cooking grease into a can and put it in the trash.
  • Scrape plates before washing.
  • Flush hot water down the kitchen drain after each use.
  • Keep wipes, floss and paper towels out of toilets.

If one drain keeps slowing, the line may be coated or partly blocked. See our drain cleaning service.

When the Drain Needs a Cable or a Jet.

A cable opens a hole through a blockage. A hydro jetting machine scrubs the full pipe wall with water. Both reach what vinegar cannot.

If you have tried the fizz twice and the drain is still slow, stop. Repeating it will not help, and delay lets a partial clog harden into a full one. Read about hydro jetting for repeat clogs.

Why the Smell Comes Back.

A drain that smells again a few days after a cleaning usually has a trap that is drying out, a vent that is blocked or a layer of buildup that the fizz did not reach. Pouring a cup of water into rarely used drains keeps the trap sealed.

If the smell is a sewer smell, the cause may be a dry floor drain in the basement. Fill it with water and a spoonful of mineral oil to slow evaporation.

What to Do if the Sink Backs Up Anyway.

If water rises instead of draining after a treatment, stop. Do not add more water or more product. Pull the stopper and bail out what you can, then check the trap under the sink for a plug of hair or grease.

If the trap is clear and the sink still will not drain, the blockage is further down the line. That is a cable job, and our drain cleaning service handles it any hour.

Safer Habits Between Cleanings.

The best drain treatment is prevention. Use a hair catcher in the shower, wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing and run hot water for a short time after using the kitchen sink.

Never mix vinegar with a commercial drain cleaner. The combination can release harmful fumes, and splashing a strong chemical can burn skin and eyes.

Nearby Homes, Same Advice.

Homes in Bridgeview and Hickory Hills have the same drain basics, and the same warning applies about chemical cleaners. If a drain backs up after a fizz treatment, call for a cable.

A camera look can show what the drain is carrying.

Sources and Safety.

The Metropolitan Water Reclamation District publishes guidance on keeping grease and wipes out of drains. Mixing vinegar with a commercial cleaner can release fumes, so read the product label.

Follow the manufacturer guidelines for any chemical you use.
