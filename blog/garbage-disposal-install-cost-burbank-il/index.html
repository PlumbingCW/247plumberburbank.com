Garbage Disposal Install Cost in Burbank, IL: Price Guide

A new garbage disposal is one of the lower-cost plumbing upgrades, but the final bill varies with the unit, the wiring and the condition of the sink drain.

This guide uses national cost data to show a realistic range and explains what pushes a price up or down.

The Price Range in Plain Numbers.

HomeServe's cost guide, which summarizes figures from Fixr, This Old House and HomeGuide, reports that installing a new disposal, including labor and new plumbing connections, runs about $250 to $650. Replacing an existing unit runs about $200 to $550 in total.

These are national figures, not quotes. They are a starting point for judging an estimate in the Chicago area.

What Labor Adds.

HomeServe reports plumbers charging $45 to $150 per hour, or about $100 to $250 for the whole job, and electricians $40 to $100 per hour when new wiring is needed.

A swap.

Replacing a unit with the same mount and an existing outlet is the shortest job. It costs the least.

A first-time install.

Adding a disposal where none exists means a new drain connection, a switch and a power source. It costs more.

Repairs the job uncovers.

A corroded P-trap, a failed sink flange or damaged drain pipes can add parts and time.

Worth knowing

Why Burbank Cares Who Does the Work.

The City of Burbank Building Department says a permit is required for construction or alteration of plumbing and that all contractors must be licensed. A swap of one disposal for another is usually minor, but a new connection or new wiring may involve a permit.

The office is at 6530 W 79th Street, phone 708-599-5500, option 2. A licensed plumber can tell you what applies.

What Raises the Price.

These items change the total.

  • A more powerful or quieter unit.
  • New electrical work or a switch.
  • Corroded drain parts that need replacing.
  • A dishwasher connection that must be changed.
  • A tight or old cabinet that takes time to work in.

If the sink also drains slowly, the line may need cleaning. See drain cleaning.

Repair or Replace.

A repair can be cheaper than a new unit if the problem is minor.

Repair makes sense when.

  • The unit is jammed.
  • A drain connection is loose.
  • The reset trips but the unit works.
  • The unit is fairly new.

Replace when.

  • The housing leaks.
  • The motor is dead.
  • The unit is old and noisy.
  • Repairs are frequent.

Our garbage disposal service covers both repair and installation.

How to Compare Quotes.

A good quote lists the unit, the labor, any parts and any permit. Ask whether the price includes haul-away of the old unit and whether wiring is included.

Confirm that the price is final after the plumber looks at the sink, and that nothing starts until you approve it.

Hidden Costs That Surprise People.

The sink flange, the mounting ring and the drain connections are the usual surprises. If the old flange is corroded or the sink has a different cutout, the plumber may need new parts to seal the new unit properly.

A disposal also needs power. If the existing outlet is under the sink and not on a switch, or the circuit is shared, a licensed electrician may need to adjust it.

Features Worth Paying For.

Not every feature is worth the money. These tend to be.

  • Sound insulation for an open kitchen layout.
  • A stainless steel grinding chamber for longer life.
  • A solid warranty from the manufacturer.
  • A continuous feed design for easy daily use.
  • A jam-clearing feature such as a reversing motor.

Skip a high-horsepower unit if your household cooks lightly. It adds cost without adding benefit.

Warranty and What It Covers.

Many disposals carry a limited warranty on parts, and some cover in-home service for a period. Read the terms. Warranties often exclude damage from objects that should not be ground, such as bones or utensils.

Keep the receipt and the model number. They are what you need if the unit fails early.

Care That Extends the Life of a Disposal.

Run cold water before, during and for a short time after grinding. Feed scraps in small amounts rather than all at once. Avoid fibrous waste such as celery strings and corn husks, and keep grease out of the unit.

A smell can be handled with ice and citrus peels. A humming sound with no grinding usually means a jam, which a hex key in the bottom of the unit often frees.

Local Context for the Kitchen Line.

Kitchen grease is the main reason disposals and drains clog. In Burbank the house sewer is the owner's responsibility out to the district main, so grease that builds up in your pipe is your problem to clear.

Homes in Bridgeview and Chicago Ridge share the same kitchen habits. A disposal does not replace the habit of scraping plates and keeping grease out.

What to Do if the Disposal Jams or Smokes.

A jammed unit can overheat. Stay safe.

  • Switch off the unit and unplug it or turn off its breaker.
  • Never put a hand in the chamber.
  • Use a hex key at the bottom to free a jam.
  • Wait for the unit to cool before pressing reset.
  • Call a plumber if it smokes, leaks or trips the breaker again.

Do not use the disposal again until the cause is clear.

Sources for Cost and Safety.

Waste disposers are tested to UL 430, and cost ranges come from national guides. The drain connection follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard found in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890 and administered by the Illinois Department of Public Health.

Local prices vary with labor, parts and the condition of the existing plumbing.
