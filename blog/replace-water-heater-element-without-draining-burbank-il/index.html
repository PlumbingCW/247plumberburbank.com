Replace a Water Heater Element Without Draining: Burbank Guide

The question comes up whenever a shower runs cold on an electric heater. The short answer is that you cannot safely swap an element without lowering the water, but you rarely need to drain the whole tank.

This guide explains what a partial drain involves, how to test the element first and where Burbank water makes the job more likely.

The Honest Answer to the Search Question.

Elements are screwed into the side of the tank, and the tank is full of water. When you unscrew the element, the water level above it has nowhere to stay. Water will pour out of the opening, and in some cases it will be hot.

You do not have to empty the whole tank. You need to lower the water below the element you are changing. For the lower element that means draining most of the tank, and for the upper element it means draining a portion.

Worth knowing

Safety Before Anything Else.

Switch off the breaker for the water heater and confirm with a non-contact voltage tester that the element terminals are dead. Never rely on the thermostat setting. Electricity and water in the same space are dangerous.

If you are not comfortable working near live wiring, call a plumber or an electrician. A mistake with this job can cause shock or burns.

How the Partial Drain Swap Works.

These steps outline the usual sequence.

1

Power off and test.

Turn off the breaker and confirm there is no voltage at the element.

2

Lower the water.

Shut the cold supply, attach a hose to the drain valve and drain until the water is below the element.

3

Swap the element.

Remove the cover, disconnect the wires, unscrew the element with an element wrench and install the new one with a new gasket.

4

Refill before power.

Close the drain, open a hot tap and refill. Restore power only after water flows steadily from the tap.

Worth knowing

Never Power a Dry Element.

A heating element in air burns out in seconds. After the tank is refilled, leave a hot tap open until water runs without sputtering, then turn the breaker on.

This is the most common reason a new element fails on day one.

Burbank Water Chemistry and Element Life.

Chicago supplies Lake Michigan water to the South Stickney Sanitary District. In 2013 quarterly lab reports, Chicago listed hardness of 134 to 143 mg/L as calcium carbonate and pH between 7.67 and 8.35. Those are 2013 numbers, so ask the district for the current report.

Moderately hard water forms scale on submerged elements, and scale insulates the metal until it overheats and fails. Flushing the tank once a year slows the buildup, and a softener removes the source.

Source: City of Chicago Department of Water Management quarterly water quality reports (2013).

When It Is Time to Call for Help.

Some situations call for a plumber instead of a repair.

  • The tank is leaking from the body or the bottom.
  • The element will not unscrew or the threads are damaged.
  • The heater is more than a decade old and both elements are scaled.
  • You see rust or sediment in the drained water.
  • The breaker trips again after the new element goes in.

Testing an Element With a Multimeter.

Test before you buy a part. A good element reads a specific resistance, and a bad one reads open or shorted.

1

Power off.

Switch off the breaker and confirm no voltage at the terminals.

2

Disconnect a lead.

Remove one wire from the element so the reading is not affected by the circuit.

3

Read resistance.

Touch the meter probes to the two terminals. A reading in a few ohms is typical, and no reading means the element is open.

4

Test to ground.

Touch one probe to a terminal and the other to the tank. Any reading means the element is shorted to ground.

When to Call Instead of Doing It Yourself.

If the element tests bad, replacing it is a manageable job for some homeowners. If the tank leaks, the wiring is damaged or the heater trips the breaker repeatedly, the problem is bigger than an element.

Electric work with water nearby carries real risk. A plumber can confirm the diagnosis, and our water heater repair service covers the work if you would rather not do it.

Preventing the Next Failure.

Flush the tank yearly to remove sediment. Check the anode rod every few years, because a spent rod lets the tank corrode. Set the thermostat at a moderate temperature, since higher settings speed scale.

Homes in Oak Lawn and Chicago Ridge draw similar water, so the same advice applies.

Safety and Standards.

Electric water heaters follow UL 174, and heating elements are rated by wattage and voltage printed on the old part. Match both when buying a replacement.

Turn off the breaker and confirm no power with a tester before touching a terminal. Working on a live element is a shock hazard.
