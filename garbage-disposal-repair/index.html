Disposal Humming or Dead? Check These First.

Turn off the wall switch and, if you can reach it, unplug the unit or flip its breaker before putting anything near the opening. Never reach into a disposal, even when it is off.

Many units have a small red reset button on the bottom. Pressing it after a jam sometimes restores power. If the unit still hums, hold the line and call, because a plumber can free the blades with the right tool in a few minutes.

Installing a New Disposal.

A replacement disposal is a short job when the sink drain and wiring are in good order.

Choosing a unit.

Horsepower and feed type matter. A household with heavy cooking benefits from a stronger motor and a continuous-feed unit.

Mounting and wiring.

The old unit is removed, the mounting ring is checked and the new disposal is wired and connected to the drain and, where present, the dishwasher.

Testing.

We run water through the unit, check every joint for leaks and confirm the dishwasher drain still works.

What Not to Put Down a Disposal.

Most repair calls start with something that should have gone in the trash.

  • Grease, fat and oil, which harden in the drain pipe.
  • Fibrous food such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels that swell and form a paste.
  • Bones, fruit pits and non-food objects.

If a clog has formed beyond the disposal, see our drain cleaning service for the kitchen line.

Kitchen Grease and the Sewer Line.

The pipe leaving a kitchen carries grease that cools and hardens. In Burbank, that waste flows to a house sewer owned by the homeowner and then to a main maintained by the South Stickney Sanitary District.

A disposal that is blamed for a clog is often exposing a coated kitchen branch. If a new disposal does not fix slow draining, the line behind it needs cleaning.

What to Do if the Disposal Jams or Hums.

A jammed unit can overheat. Stay safe.

  • Switch off the unit and unplug it or turn off its breaker.
  • Never put a hand in the chamber.
  • Use a hex key at the bottom to free a jam.
  • Wait for the unit to cool before pressing reset.
  • Call a plumber if it leaks or trips the breaker.

Do not use the disposal again until the cause is clear.

Safety Standard for Disposals.

Waste disposers are tested to UL 430, and the label on the unit lists its rating. Power must be off at the switch and the plug before anyone reaches near the grinding chamber.

The drain connection follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard found in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890 and administered by the Illinois Department of Public Health, and a sink with a dishwasher needs an air gap or high loop on the drain hose.
