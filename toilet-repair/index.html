Overflowing Toilet: Do This Right Now.

Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop more water entering the bowl. Then close the valve on the wall behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.

If the bowl keeps rising, the clog may be deeper than the toilet. Gurgling sinks and a bubbling floor drain mean the problem is on the main line, and the 24/7 line can send a crew for that too.

Running Toilets and Your Water Bill.

The South Stickney Sanitary District, which delivers water in Burbank, describes a ten-minute toilet dye test and says toilets are the top cause of lost water. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait ten minutes without flushing and look at the bowl.

If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper or fill valve is leaking. That is an inexpensive repair that protects against a high bill.

Installing a New Toilet.

A replacement toilet is a straightforward job when the floor and the flange are in good shape.

Check the flange first.

The flange is the ring that connects the toilet to the drain. If it is cracked, rusted or sits below the floor surface, it needs repair before the new toilet can seal.

Choose a compliant model.

Burbank requires WaterSense-labeled new and replacement fixtures under ordinances the Illinois Department of Public Health approved in 2016. We install models that meet the requirement.

Set and test.

The toilet is set on a new wax ring or seal, the supply line is replaced, and the unit is tested for leaks and flush performance before we leave.

Signs the Floor Needs a Closer Look.

A leak at the toilet can hide damage under the flooring. These signs call for a deeper check.

  • A soft or spongy spot around the toilet base.
  • Staining on the ceiling below a second-floor bathroom.
  • A musty smell that returns after cleaning.
  • Floor tile that is loose or lifting near the toilet.
  • Water that reappears after you tighten the bolts.

If any of these apply, tell dispatch. A plumber can check the subfloor and the flange before setting a new toilet.

Standards Behind Toilet Parts.

Toilet bowls are made to ASME A112.19.2, and the EPA WaterSense label marks models rated at 1.28 gallons per flush or less. Burbank ordinances approved by the Illinois Department of Public Health include a WaterSense requirement.

If you are choosing a new toilet, look for that label on the box.
