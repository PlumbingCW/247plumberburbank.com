Pump Stopped and Water Is Rising.

If the pit is filling and the pump is silent, check that it is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped. Look at the float, since a stuck float is a frequent cause and sometimes frees with a gentle push.

If the pump still does not run, call the 24/7 line. A bucket or a wet vacuum can buy time while a plumber is on the way, and moving belongings off the floor protects what you can.

Primary Pump or Backup System.

Many basements have only a primary pump. The two systems protect against different failures.

Primary pump.

  • Runs on household power.
  • Handles normal groundwater.
  • Fails in a power outage.
  • Wears out with use.

Battery or water-powered backup.

  • Runs when the power fails.
  • Takes over if the primary jams.
  • Needs a battery that is tested and replaced.
  • Adds a second discharge path.

Pair a new primary with a backup for the best protection during storms.

What We Repair and Install.

A sump system has more parts than the pump.

Pump and float.

We replace pumps that have burned out, jammed or lost their float switch, and we test them in the pit before leaving.

Check valve and discharge pipe.

A check valve stops pumped water from flowing back into the pit. The discharge pipe must carry water away from the house.

Basin and cover.

A cracked or undersized pit shortens pump life. A sealed cover keeps radon, moisture and debris out of the basement.

Worth knowing

Where the Discharge Must Go.

The South Stickney Sanitary District states that sump pumps must not be connected to the sanitary sewer. Pumped groundwater and roof water in the sanitary system can overload the sewer and push sewage into basements.

The pipe should discharge outside, well away from the foundation. We follow that rule on every installation.

A new sump install has four stages.

Codes and Standards for Sump Pumps.

Sump pumps are tested to UL 778. Burbank prohibits discharge from a sump pump into the sanitary sewer, so the discharge line has to go to an approved location outdoors.

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan identifies flooding as a recurring risk in the region, which is why a battery backup is worth discussing.

What to Do if the Pit Fills and the Pump Fails.

A failed sump pump during a storm is an emergency. These steps keep people safe and limit damage.

  • Do not walk through standing water if the basement has an electrical panel or outlets nearby.
  • Move stored items to higher shelves if you can do so safely.
  • Unplug the pump only if the floor around it is dry.
  • Use a wet vacuum only on outlets that are away from water.
  • Call dispatch and describe the water level and whether the pit is running.

Do not enter a flooded basement if the water is rising toward electrical equipment.
