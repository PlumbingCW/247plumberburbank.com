Every Drain Is Slow at Once.

A single slow sink is a branch problem. When the tub, the washing machine and the toilets are all slow or backing up together, the shared main line is blocked. It is the pipe that carries everything out of the house to the street.

Stop running water and call the 24/7 line. Dispatch will ask where your cleanout is and whether the floor drain in the basement is bubbling, because both answers shape the visit.

Main-line cleaning follows a set order.

What Blocks a Main Sewer Line.

The cause decides how often the line needs service afterward.

Tree roots.

Fine roots enter at joints and catch paper. Cleaning clears them, but they regrow unless the joint is repaired.

Grease and wipes.

Cooking fats harden in the pipe, and so-called flushable wipes knit together and snag on rough spots.

Scale on old iron.

Mineral buildup narrows the bore of older cast iron until a small clog is enough to stop flow.

A belly in the pipe.

A sagging section holds standing water. Solids settle there and form a recurring blockage.

The District Main Versus Your Main Line.

The sewer main under the street belongs to the South Stickney Sanitary District. The pipe from your house to that main is the homeowner's, and that is the line we clean.

If your cleaning crew finds the line clear and the water still will not move, the blockage is further down in the district main. The district's number is 708-424-6030, and calling it with our findings speeds the response.

Cleaning Now or Inspecting First.

Both are useful. The order depends on how urgent the backup is.

Clean first.

  • Restores service fastest.
  • Best when sewage is already in the house.
  • Followed by a camera pass.
  • Good for a first-time backup.

Inspect first.

  • Shows the cause before any force is used.
  • Best for repeat backups.
  • Prevents jetting a cracked pipe.
  • Part of a maintenance plan.

How to Keep the Main Line Clear.

Maintenance habits cut the number of emergency calls.

  • Keep grease out of the kitchen drain and throw it away in the trash.
  • Flush toilet paper only, and bin wipes, even those labeled flushable.
  • Locate the cleanout and keep it accessible.
  • Schedule a camera check if the line has clogged twice within a year.
  • Keep the downspouts and sump pump discharge away from the sanitary sewer.

Burbank's sanitary district states that sump pumps must not be connected to the sanitary sewer, since the extra water can overload the line.

What to Do When Sewage Backs Up.

Sewage carries bacteria. These steps limit exposure until the crew arrives.

  • Stop running water and flushing at once.
  • Keep children and pets out of the affected room.
  • Wear gloves and boots if you must enter.
  • Do not mix cleaning chemicals to treat the spill.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer before cleanup.

Call dispatch and say which drains are backing up.

Who Owns the Pipe and What the Rules Say.

The South Stickney Sanitary District maintains the sewer main, and the owner is responsible beyond it. The Metropolitan Water Reclamation District publishes guidance on what should and should not go into a sewer.

Cleaning a private line needs no permit, but a repair does under the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard found in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890 and administered by the Illinois Department of Public Health.
