Stop the Water First.

Look under the sink for the small oval-handled valves on the hot and cold lines, and turn each one clockwise until it stops. If the faucet has no shutoff or the valve itself is leaking, close the main water shutoff, which is usually in the basement near the meter.

Once the water is stopped, call the line. Dispatch will ask about the faucet brand if you know it, and the kind of leak: from the spout, the handle or the base.

Where a Faucet Leaks and What It Means.

The leak point tells the plumber what part has failed.

Drips from the spout.

A worn cartridge, washer or seat. The parts are inexpensive and the fix is quick.

Leaks around the handle.

Worn O-rings or a loose packing nut. Common on older two-handle faucets.

Water at the base of the faucet.

A failed seal or a cracked body. Often a sign the faucet needs replacement.

Water under the sink.

A bad supply line, a failed stop valve or a loose drain connection. These can flood a cabinet quickly.

Fixtures and Valves We Repair.

Faucet repair covers more than the kitchen sink.

Kitchen and bath faucets.

We replace cartridges, aerators and seats, and swap the whole faucet when parts are not worth the cost.

Shower and tub valves.

A shower that will not turn off or a tub spout that runs usually has a worn cartridge inside the wall valve. Some repairs need no wall access.

Stop valves and supply lines.

Old stop valves seize and leak when turned. We replace them along with braided supply lines so the next repair is easier.

Laundry and utility sinks.

Washing machine valves and utility sink faucets fail the same ways and often flood basements when they do.

Hard Water and Faucet Wear.

Chicago, which supplies water to the district serving Burbank, reported hardness of 134 to 143 mg/L as calcium carbonate in its 2013 lab reports. That is a 2013 figure, so ask the water utility for the current one.

Moderately hard water leaves mineral buildup in aerators and cartridges, which causes weak flow and sticky handles. Cleaning the aerator is a quick maintenance task, and a cartridge change usually resets the problem.

Winter Spigots and Frozen Outdoor Lines.

South Stickney Sanitary District advises that a frozen service line can be shut off at the curb box near the street, which the district calls a Buffalo Box. If an outdoor spigot froze and split, shut the interior valve that feeds it and call dispatch before the thaw.

A split spigot sprays water inside the wall when the ice melts. Closing the supply early limits the damage.

Standards for Faucets and Supply Lines.

Faucets are made to ASME A112.18.1, and lead-free requirements are set by NSF/ANSI 372. A replacement faucet should carry both marks on the box.

We use braided supply lines with a reliable pressure rating, and we tell you if the shutoff valve under the sink is too worn to trust.
