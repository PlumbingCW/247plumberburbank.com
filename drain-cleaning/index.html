What to Do When a Drain Backs Up Tonight.

Stop running water first. Every sink, shower, dishwasher and washing machine in the house shares one drain system, so more water only pushes the backup higher. If sewage is coming up through a basement floor drain, keep people and pets away from it and call the 24/7 line.

Tell dispatch which fixtures are slow and which are fully blocked. One slow tub points to a branch line. Gurgling toilets plus a flooded floor drain point to the main line that leaves the house, which is a different job with different equipment.

Match the Symptom to the Likely Clog.

The pattern of what is backing up tells a plumber where to start. These are the patterns we hear about most from Burbank homes.

One fixture is slow or blocked.

Usually hair, soap scum or grease in that fixture's own branch line. A cable through the trap or cleanout normally reaches it.

Several fixtures on one floor back up.

A shared branch is blocked further down. Expect a longer cable run, and sometimes a camera look to see what is sitting in the pipe.

Basement floor drain overflows.

The main house drain is blocked or the sewer main is surcharged. This is where the question of who owns the pipe matters, covered below.

Toilet gurgles when a sink drains.

Air is being pushed through a partly blocked line. It often shows up before a full backup, so treat it as an early warning.

Who Owns the Blocked Pipe in Burbank?

In Burbank the South Stickney Sanitary District (SSSD) maintains the public sanitary sewer mains. The district's own guidance says property owners are responsible for the line from the house out to the main, including the connection under the parkway.

That split decides who you call first when water backs up into the basement.

Snaking or Hydro Jetting: Which Cleans the Drain?

Most clogs clear with a cable. Jetting is the better tool when the line keeps clogging after snaking.

Cable (snaking).

  • Opens a hole through the blockage.
  • Fast for hair, paper and a single obstruction.
  • Right for sinks, tubs and short branch lines.
  • Can leave grease on the pipe wall.

Hydro jetting.

  • Scours the full pipe wall with high-pressure water.
  • Clears grease, scale and root hairs.
  • Right for repeat clogs and main lines.
  • Needs a camera check first on old pipe.

Drain Types We Clear.

Every drain in a house has its own habits. Knowing which one is failing shortens the visit.

Kitchen sink drains.

Grease and food waste harden inside the line, so a kitchen sink that drains slowly after every dishwasher cycle usually has a coated pipe, not a single clog. A cable opens it and jetting cleans it.

Tub and shower drains.

Hair and soap build a plug just below the strainer or in the trap. Many of these clear in a short visit without opening the wall.

Basement floor drains and laundry standpipes.

These sit lowest in the house, so they show the first sign of a main-line blockage. If a floor drain bubbles when the washer runs, the problem is almost never the drain itself.

Do This Before the Technician Arrives.

A few minutes of preparation saves time on site and limits damage.

  • Stop using water in every fixture until the line is clear.
  • Keep chemical drain cleaner out of the pipe, because it can burn a technician and slow the repair.
  • Move rugs, boxes and stored items away from the basement floor drain.
  • Note which fixtures back up and in what order.
  • Take a photo of any standing water for insurance records.

If the water is rising quickly, shut off the main water supply. The shutoff is usually in the basement near the meter.

Codes Behind Drain Work.

Drain and vent work in Illinois falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard found in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890 and administered by the Illinois Department of Public Health. Burbank has its own approved ordinances adopting the plumbing rules, and the City of Burbank Building Department issues permits when a repair changes the pipe layout.

A cable or jetting visit usually needs no permit. Replacing a section of drain does, and we say so before the work starts.
